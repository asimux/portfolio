Where people were leaving

Register opened a browser.
Most people didn't follow it.

For an app asking people to trust it with their savings, it looked a lot like a phishing page. People treated it like one.

The catch

I couldn't change the form

Bringing sign-up into the app didn't mean I could design it however I liked. The KYC API still did the verification behind the scenes, and it wanted six stages, in this order, with its own validation rules.

My first version grouped things the way a new user would expect. Engineering showed me it wouldn't pass, so I threw it away and started again from the API. I kept its order and decided everything else: how much goes on each screen, and what kind of input each answer needs.

The other catch

The UAE rules for gold dealers

UAE anti-money-laundering law treats gold dealers as high risk, and the data protection law is strict about consent. The API decided the order. These rules decided what I had to ask, and how.

What I gave up

Four calls I'd make again

Account first, the API's order after

Following the API to the letter meant asking about someone's income before they even had an account. So you create the account first, then the API's stages run in order. It relies on the API accepting the email we already verified. If they don't, one small "confirm your email" screen comes back.

A longer flow, told upfront

The rules added an address screen and four more fields, and I couldn't cut them. Instead I tell people what they'll need and roughly how long it takes, and show a seven-step progress bar the whole way.

Two extra taps for consent

A pre-ticked box is smoother, but here it doesn't count as consent. Both the terms and the selfie consent start empty, and the selfie button waits for you.

Good ideas left out

Investment goals and US tax questions are normal in big fintech apps. The API's form had nowhere to put them, so they wait until compliance decides on FATCA or CRS.

Testing it

Back to the same people

When I interviewed people about the old flow, I asked if they'd try the redesign later, and they agreed. So the people testing the new version were the same ones who had told me what was broken.
