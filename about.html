A moment I'm proud of

In 2021, I walked into my first design role and found the team designing in Photoshop.

PSDs everywhere. Flat files passed between designers and developers with no shared source of truth. Figma was already well established, but the company hadn't made the switch.

I didn't just suggest Figma. I got the stakeholders on board by showing them how much faster and more collaborative the workflow could be for designing UIs, building components and handing off to engineering. Then I did the hard part: migrating every existing PSD into Figma, file by file, and onboarding the whole team onto a new way of working.

By the time I left WPBrigade three years later, Photoshop was gone from the design workflow entirely. The team worked in a proper design system with tokens, variants and auto layout, and handoff time had dropped 40%.

It taught me something I still carry: the best design work isn't always on screen. Sometimes it's changing how a team works.

Process

How I work

1

Talk to real people first

I start with user interviews and stakeholder sessions, not assumptions. Before I open Figma I want to know what's broken, what's confusing and what people actually need.

2

Sketch fast, decide slow

Low-fidelity wireframes and flows come first. I explore several directions, share early and drop ideas that don't hold up. The best answer is rarely the first one.

3

Stay with it until it ships

High-fidelity screens, components and specs engineers can build from. I stay involved through development, because the work isn't done until it's live and measured.
